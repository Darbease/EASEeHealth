## Clinical Summary

Ms. Garcia is a 57-year-old woman with an 18-month history of progressive right knee pain localized to the medial joint line. The pain is mechanical in nature, worsens with weight-bearing and stair climbing, and now limits ambulation to less than two blocks. She reports intermittent locking and a sensation of instability.

Conservative management over the past four months has included a supervised physical-therapy program (12 sessions), activity modification, scheduled NSAIDs (ibuprofen 800 mg), and an intra-articular corticosteroid injection on 2026-02-15 — all with only transient relief.

Weight-bearing radiographs demonstrate medial-compartment joint-space narrowing without acute osseous abnormality. Examination shows medial joint-line tenderness, a small effusion, and a positive McMurray sign, raising concern for an internal derangement (meniscal tear) superimposed on the underlying osteoarthritis.

## Rationale for MRI

MRI of the knee without contrast is the appropriate next diagnostic step to evaluate for a meniscal or chondral lesion that cannot be assessed on plain films, and to guide a possible arthroscopic intervention. The combination of failed conservative therapy of ≥6 weeks, mechanical symptoms, and a positive provocative examination meets standard payer criteria for advanced knee imaging.

The requested study (CPT 73721) is consistent with the member's plan coverage for diagnostic knee imaging and is below the plan's per-study imaging cap. Approval is requested.
